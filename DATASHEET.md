# Datasheet — FieldBench Corpus

Following *Datasheets for Datasets* (Gebru et al., 2021). This documents the corpus's
composition, provenance, labeling, intended uses, and limitations. Read it before
drawing conclusions from any accuracy number produced on this corpus.

## Motivation

**Why was it created?** Document-extraction systems routinely claim 95%+ accuracy, but
there is no cross-domain, field-level, publicly-licensed benchmark at scale to check
such claims. This corpus provides one: schema-driven extraction (document → structured
JSON) across many domains, with per-field ground truth and a type-aware scorer, so
accuracy claims become falsifiable and comparable.

**Who created it?** The FieldBench maintainers. Schemas and tooling are the maintainers'
own work; **real-document ground truth was generated by the maintainers' AI extraction
pipeline**, not hand-labeled (see § Preprocessing / cleaning / labeling). Source
documents are credited in `ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Composition

- **1,441 documents**, 10 categories, **768 real / 673 synthetic** (real-majority).
- **9,768 ground-truth field slots**, of which **7,691 are non-null** values (the rest
  are deliberate nulls — a field a correct system should decline to fill).
- Categories: sec_filings (406), receipts (171), invoices (155), insurance_claims (152),
  contracts (140), medical_records (140), legal_filings (99), insurance_policies (97),
  insurance_certificates (61), irs_forms (20).

**Input composition — the load-bearing caveat (see `docs/composition.md`):** most
documents never existed as a rendered page. Only **143 of 1,441 (9.9%) came from an image
or PDF**; 357 (24.8%) are machine-readable HTML→markdown; 941 (65.3%) never had a page
(plain text or authored markdown). **For ~90% of this corpus, the benchmark measures
extraction from clean text, not from rendered documents** — parse-stage failures (OCR
error, layout collapse, table-structure loss) are largely absent by construction. Scope
every accuracy claim accordingly. Adding a rendered-page (PDF/scan) slice is the primary
planned expansion.

**Real vs. synthetic.** Real documents come from public sources (below). Synthetic
documents are generated by the maintainers to cover specific extraction patterns and
edge cases. All results should be reported stratified by `source` (real / synthetic);
synthetic documents systematically overestimate accuracy relative to real ones.

**Matched-pair subset.** Five categories (`medical_records`, `legal_filings`,
`contracts`, `sec_filings`, `receipts`) are **dual-source**: alongside their real
documents, they carry synthetic documents generated against the *same schema*, so the
synthetic-vs-real gap can be measured *holding category constant*. The released corpus
ships the realistic level (realistic prose with distractors) — **200 documents, 40 per
category** — tagged `matched_pair: true` and `synthetic_realism: 1`. A clean/templated
baseline (`synthetic_realism: 0`) is a trivially-extractable strawman for
synthetic-realism-gradient analysis and is **not** shipped by default; regenerate the
matched pairs deterministically with `scripts/wire_matched_pairs.py` (add
`--include-clean` to also emit level 0). Because the shipped matched pairs raise the
synthetic share to ~47%, **never quote a pooled (source-blind) accuracy** — stratify.

## Collection process

Real documents were collected from public sources:
- **SEC EDGAR** (sec_filings, contracts) — mandatory public disclosures.
- **Caselaw Access Project** (legal_filings) — public-domain U.S. court opinions.
- **MTSamples** (medical_records) — de-identified sample medical transcriptions.
  Distributed with patient identifiers already replaced by placeholders (e.g. "ABCD
  Hospital", "Dr. A"). We additionally scanned all 100 real clinical notes for residual
  structured identifiers — social-security numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, and
  record numbers — and found none; names and institutions appear only as placeholders.
- **SROIE / ICDAR 2019** (receipts) — CC BY 4.0.
- **U.S. federal & state government forms** (insurance_claims) — public-domain forms.

Synthetic documents (invoices, insurance certificates/policies, IRS forms, and the
matched-pair sets) are generated by deterministic scripts in `scripts/sources/`.

## Preprocessing / cleaning / labeling

- **Representation.** Source documents are parsed to markdown (the representation the
  benchmark scores against). Images/PDFs via OCR/parse engines; HTML via HTML→markdown;
  text as-is; synthetic authored directly as markdown.
- **Ground truth.** Synthetic documents carry ground truth *by construction* (the
  generator emits the field values it rendered). Real-document ground truth was
  **generated by an AI extraction pipeline** — directed and configured by the
  maintainers against each category's schema, but not hand-labeled or
  exhaustively human-verified. The inter-annotator agreement pass (below) is the
  first independent human check on it; see
  [`docs/annotation-guidelines.md`](docs/annotation-guidelines.md).
- **Inter-annotator agreement.** [PENDING v1.0] A stratified sample of real
  documents is annotated by independent humans and their agreement with the
  AI-produced ground truth is reported (`scripts/iaa_harness.py`; rules in
  [`docs/annotation-guidelines.md`](docs/annotation-guidelines.md)). Until that
  number is published, treat real-document ground truth as **AI-generated and
  not yet independently validated**.
- **Null semantics.** A field absent from a document is annotated `null`; the scorer
  distinguishes a correct null (correct-absence) from a hallucination.

## Uses

- **Intended:** benchmarking and comparing schema-driven document-extraction systems;
  measuring the real-vs-synthetic accuracy gap; studying null/hallucination behavior.
- **Not intended:** training clinical, legal, or financial decision systems; the medical
  documents are de-identified samples, not real patient records, and must not be used as
  clinical references.
- **Known risk:** synthetic documents overestimate accuracy; do not report a
  synthetic-inclusive number without the real/synthetic split.

## Distribution

- Hosted at `github.com/fieldbench/corpus` (and a HuggingFace mirror + Zenodo DOI on
  release). Schemas, tooling, and ground truth are freely licensed as the maintainers'
  output (real-document GT is AI-generated — see § Preprocessing).
- **Per-source licenses** (see `ATTRIBUTION.md`): synthetic — CC0; SEC EDGAR — public
  disclosure; SROIE — CC BY 4.0 (attribution required); MTSamples — educational-use with
  attribution; Caselaw Access Project — public domain; government forms — public
  domain. ACORD/ISO copyrighted insurance form layouts are **not** included; those
  categories use functionally-equivalent synthetic documents.

## Maintenance

- **Versioned.** Releases are versioned (v1.0, v1.1, …); citations point at a version/DOI
  so expansion does not break references.
- **Planned expansion (post-v1.0):** a rendered-page (PDF/scan) slice to close the
  clean-text composition gap; more real documents per category; published IAA.
- **Contact / issues:** via the GitHub repository.

## Limitations (summary)

1. **~90% extraction-from-clean-text**, not rendered-document extraction (composition table).
2. **~47% synthetic** (five categories are dual-source, matched-pair) — always report stratified by source; never pool.
3. **AI-generated real GT, not yet independently human-validated** until the IAA pass is published.
4. **English-language business/legal/medical/financial documents** only.
5. Schemas were co-designed with the maintainers' extraction pipeline (a potential confound
   for that pipeline; independent systems are unaffected).
